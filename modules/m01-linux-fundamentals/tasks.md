# Linux Fundamentals Tasks

**Objective:** Explain what happens between typing a command and seeing output, using your own system
as the evidence.

> There is no solutions file for this stage. This checklist guides the work — it doesn't contain it. See
> `brief.md` for the full objective, scope, stack constraints, and definition of done, and fill in
> `write-up-template.md` as you go, not after.

## Setup

- [ ] Confirm with your trainer: how much detail your observation notes need before you begin.

## Build

- [ ] Research the "Research:" questions under each Scope bullet in `brief.md` before you start
      investigating, and jot down what you expect to find.
- [ ] Investigate the kernel, the shell, and your distribution, and trace how a typed command travels
      from the shell to the kernel and back as output. Record what you find as you go.
- [ ] Investigate your system's kernel and distribution identity directly from the command line, and
      record the real output — not a paraphrase.
- [ ] Explore the filesystem as an abstraction, and note what distinguishes it from the physical storage
      underneath.
- [ ] Investigate what's actually running on your system right now, and note anything that differs from
      what you expected before you looked.
- [ ] Compile your written observation notes from the investigations above into one coherent record.

## Verify

- [ ] Confirm your notes name your system's actual kernel version and distribution, not generic facts
      copied from documentation.
- [ ] Confirm every claim in your notes is backed by real terminal output you captured, not written from
      memory.
- [ ] Practice answering, unprompted: "what happens between typing a command and seeing output?" using
      this module's concepts — this is also the course's self-check at the M1–M2 boundary.

## Write-up

- [ ] Under "What I built," describe your observation notes and what they cover.
- [ ] Under "Why it's built this way," record which Scope questions you researched, what you chose to
      inspect and in what order, and what surprised you about what was actually running.
- [ ] Under "How to build it," write a guide for someone who has never looked under the hood of their
      own machine before.
- [ ] Under "Concepts worth explaining," pick 1–2 ideas (kernel vs. shell vs. distro, or the filesystem
      as abstraction) and explain each in your own words.
- [ ] Under "What tripped me up," capture anything that confused you along the way.
- [ ] Under "Checkpoint evidence," include the real terminal output you used as evidence for your notes.
- [ ] Under "What I'd do differently," reflect on what you'd check first instead.
- [ ] Final self-review: re-read your notes and write-up against M1's definition of done, and confirm
      you can answer the kernel-to-output question unprompted.
