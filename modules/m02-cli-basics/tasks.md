# CLI Basics Tasks

**Objective:** Compose commands together — not just run them one at a time — to produce and reshape
output exactly how you want it.

> There is no solutions file for this stage. This checklist guides the work — it doesn't contain it. See
> `brief.md` for the full objective, scope, stack constraints, and definition of done, and fill in
> `write-up-template.md` as you go, not after.

## Setup

- [ ] Confirm with your trainer: what counts as a real command and data set to filter, if you're unsure.
- [ ] Choose the real data on your own system that you'll filter, rather than a toy example.

## Build

- [ ] Research the "Research:" questions under each Scope bullet in `brief.md` before you start.
- [ ] Run commands with options and arguments, and note how each part changes the output.
- [ ] Chain at least two commands with a pipe, reading the pipeline left to right.
- [ ] Redirect output to a file, and try both overwrite and append so you see the difference.
- [ ] Use `man`, `--help`, and tab-completion to find something you didn't already know.
- [ ] Produce your redirected file listing and record the exact command that creates it.

## Verify

- [ ] Confirm the deliverable file exists on disk and re-running your documented command reproduces its
      contents.
- [ ] Confirm your notes say when you used overwrite vs. append, and why.
- [ ] Practice explaining, unprompted, something `man` or tab-completion told you that you didn't
      already know.

## Write-up

- [ ] Under "What I built," describe the command(s) you composed and the file they produced.
- [ ] Under "Why it's built this way," explain why this command and data, and why overwrite vs. append.
- [ ] Under "How to build it," write a guide to composing commands with pipes and redirection, using
      your own example to show the reasoning.
- [ ] Under "Concepts worth explaining," pick 1–2 ideas (pipes, redirection, or how `man` and
      tab-completion change how you work) and explain each in your own words.
- [ ] Under "What tripped me up," capture anything that didn't behave as expected the first time.
- [ ] Under "Checkpoint evidence," show the exact command(s) and the resulting file's contents.
- [ ] Under "What I'd do differently," reflect on what you'd change if you started over.
- [ ] Final self-review: re-read your notes and write-up against M2's definition of done.
